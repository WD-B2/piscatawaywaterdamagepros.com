Before anything else

Safety steps while water is still moving

Stay out of moving water

Even shallow flowing water can knock a person down and hide a washed-out road. Do not walk or drive through it.

Do not go downstairs into rising water

A wet panel or furnace can electrify the water. Wait for the utility or a professional.

Keep people and pets away

Brook and street water can carry sewage and chemicals. Keep children and pets out of it.

Move valuables up

Lift boxes and electronics off the floor while the stairs are still safe to use.

Flood maps and insurance

Standard homeowners policies often exclude flood, while a sudden pipe failure is frequently covered. Your policy and your insurer decide. Check your declarations page for National Flood Insurance Program or private flood cover. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Piscataway has been in the NFIP since January 18, 1984, and the FEMA flood map service lets you look up your own address.

Keep every receipt for emergency work such as water removal, because insurers ask for them, and take photographs before cleanup begins. If the brook is rising, local and county emergency notices are the place to check for road closures before you travel.

After the water is out

Drying decides how the job ends. Mold can begin to grow on materials that stay damp, and the EPA advises drying within 24 to 48 hours. We log readings daily so you can see the numbers fall. If a wet area smells musty or has visible growth, ask us about mold remediation. We do not make health claims about mold, and a physician is the right person for those questions.
