Musty Smell After Water Damage: A Piscataway Guide

A musty odor after water damage usually means something is still wet. Finding it is the whole job.

Homeowner guidePiscataway, NJ
24/7 emergency line(732) 352-9660 Call now

By the Piscataway Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 13, 2026.

A smell is a clue, not a cleaning problem

A musty smell after water damage almost always points to moisture that is still present: wet padding, damp insulation, a soaked wall cavity or a slab that has not dried. Air fresheners and candles hide it, and they leave the cause in place. The practical way to remove the smell is to find and dry the source.

This guide helps you track down the likely spot, decide what you can do yourself and know when to call for meters and thermal imaging.

Where the smell tends to come from

Likely sourceClueUsual fix
Carpet paddingSmell strongest at the floor, worse in humid weatherRemove and replace padding, dry subfloor
Wall cavitySmell near a baseboard or an old stainOpen, dry or replace wet material
InsulationSmell near a ceiling or in a crawl spaceRemove wet insulation
Concrete slab or blockCool, damp basement airDehumidify and dry
HVAC ducts or a drain panSmell when the system runsHVAC cleaning and drain fix

Start with your nose and your hands. Walk the area slowly and note where the smell is strongest. Press on baseboards and wall bottoms for softness. Lift a corner of carpet if you can do so safely. If you find a wet spot, you have found the lead.

What you can do right away

Dry the area

Run a dehumidifier and keep windows closed in humid weather.

Remove soft goods

Pull out wet rugs, cardboard and stored fabrics.

Check for a source

Look for a leak at pipes, appliances and roof edges.

Avoid masking

Fresheners can hide the problem while it gets worse.

If the smell is faint and fades once you dry a damp spot, you may be finished. If it returns, the moisture is hidden. Moisture meters read inside walls without cutting them, and thermal cameras show cool wet areas as color. Those tools are why a crew can often find a source in minutes after hours of searching by hand.

Odor after a past water loss

Odors can linger after a loss if drying was incomplete. Padding left in place, drywall that dried on the surface but not inside and insulation that never came out all keep smell alive. In those cases the smell is not a new problem. It is the old one, still there. Structural drying and dehumidification explains how we confirm that a structure is dry, and carpet water damage restoration covers padding decisions.

We make no health claims about mold or odors. If someone in your home is reacting to the air, talk to a physician and dry the source regardless.

When it may be mold

A musty or earthy smell with a visible stain is a sign of possible growth. Growth is a result of moisture, so drying comes first and removal follows when needed. Our mold remediation page explains containment and removal, and we do not rely on air testing to decide in obvious cases.

Older homes smell differently

In Highland Park, about 80 percent of homes were built before 1979. Older plaster, lath and original insulation can hold moisture and odor for years after a leak. If your home is that age, a slow, hidden source behind a wall is a common reason a smell returns after cleaning.

What a crew does when you call about a smell

Odor calls start with questions. When did the water event happen, where is the smell strongest, and does it change with weather or when the HVAC runs? Those answers narrow the search before anyone arrives. On site, the crew walks the area, takes readings with a pin or pinless moisture meter and uses a thermal camera to find cool, wet areas that look dry.

If readings are high, the next step is access. That might be a small opening in drywall, a lifted section of carpet or a look into a crawl space. If padding or insulation is wet, it is removed. Then drying equipment is set, and the smell usually fades within days as the material dries, though some odors require cleaning of surfaces too.

If the readings are normal and the smell remains, the source may be in HVAC, a drain trap or a stored item. We tell you honestly when the answer is not a water problem.

When the smell is not water

Not every musty smell is a water problem. Stored cardboard, old books, a dirty HVAC filter and a dry floor drain trap can all smell stale. A quick test is to pour a cup of water into each unused floor drain, since a dry trap lets sewer gas rise. Replace the furnace filter and run the fan for an hour. If the smell fades, the cause was air, not water.

If neither helps, return to the moisture search. Check the base of exterior walls, the area behind the washer and the closet nearest the water heater. Those three spots account for many of the hidden sources we find.

When a smell becomes a mold question

A persistent musty smell after a leak often means hidden moisture. ANSI/IICRC S520 is the reference for what happens next if growth is found: containment, removal of contaminated porous material, and moisture control. We make no health claims about odors. If someone in the home has symptoms, a physician is the right conversation.
