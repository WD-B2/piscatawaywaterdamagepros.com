Water damage in Edison, NJ, season by season

Edison is a township of about 106,836 people, roughly three miles northeast of our Piscataway base. The original settlement of Piscatawaytown lies inside Edison, which is why the two places share a name and a long history. With a township this size, water damage follows the calendar more than any single neighborhood.

That is why we describe Edison by season: winter freezes, spring melt and rain, summer storms, and the fall tropical remnants that can drop a foot of rain in a day.

Winter: freezes and thaw floods

NOAA data puts New Jersey at about 179 freeze-free days a year, so freezing nights run for months. Pipes in exterior walls, garages and attics freeze and split, and the flood arrives when the ice thaws. Our frozen pipe water damage page covers what to do during and after a cold snap. Keep taps dripping in hard freezes and know where your main valve is.

Spring and summer: rain, roofs and storms

The state averages about 47.6 inches of precipitation a year, and heavy downpours test roofs, gutters and foundations. In Ida on September 1 and 2, 2021, Edison recorded at least 12 inches of rain, and Middlesex County received a federal disaster declaration. Roof leaks, window leaks and flooded basements all follow that pattern, and the storm water damage cleanup page explains our response.

A leak that starts on a roof often shows inside as a ceiling stain days later, so it is worth checking the attic after any big storm. Ceiling water damage repair explains how we trace and dry those cases.

Edison ZIP codes and township offices

Our Edison coverage includes 08817, which is the primary ZIP, along with 08820, 08837 and 08899. The township's municipal complex is at 100 Municipal Boulevard, which is where larger rebuilds and permits are handled. We cover the full township, so give us the street and we confirm the ZIP when we dispatch.

Fall: tropical remnants and backed-up drains

Late summer and fall bring the heaviest single-day rain, as Ida showed in September 2021. When storm sewers fill, water can push back through floor drains and low fixtures, and that is sewage-grade water. Our sewage backup cleanup page explains how we contain and disinfect these events.

Fall is also when gutters fill with leaves, and clogged gutters push water against walls and into basements. A quick cleaning before the first big storm is one of the cheapest forms of prevention.
