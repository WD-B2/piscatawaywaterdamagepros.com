Wall repair decisions

When drywall gets wet: dry it or replace it?

Wall and drywall water damage in Piscataway shows up as stains, soft spots, bubbled paint or a hollow feel when you tap. Gypsum board wicks water upward and loses strength, so a small visible spot can hide a larger wet area.

We answer 24/7 and dispatch the same day when available. Until we arrive, find the source, shut it off and keep people from leaning on soft walls.

How drywall reacts to water

Clean water on drywall for a short time can be dried in place. Drywall that sagged, crumbled or sat wet for days usually has to be cut out. Insulation inside the wall holds water and needs to be checked, and so do studs and the bottom plate.

Before you cut or paint: a short checklist

Many wall repairs go wrong in the first hour, usually because someone paints over a stain or opens a wall without finding the source. A few simple checks save a lot of rework. They also give a restoration crew the facts needed to plan drying.

Find and stop the source

Look above the stain for a bathroom, a supply line or an exterior wall. Shut the water off if you can reach the valve.

Photograph before touching

Take dated photos of the stain, the baseboard and the floor. Insurers want to see the condition before repair.

Check for electrical risk

Outlets in a wet wall are a hazard. Keep power off to that circuit if the panel is dry and reachable.

Do not paint yet

Paint traps moisture and hides the stain, which makes the wet zone harder to measure.

Older Piscataway homes around Possumtown, Randolphville and Society Hill often have plaster over lath rather than drywall, and plaster reacts differently to water. It can hold its shape while the lath behind it stays wet, so a moisture meter matters more than how the surface looks.

What a flood cut actually is

A flood cut is a straight horizontal cut a few inches above the highest wet reading, so every wet inch of board comes out and the new board joins on dry material. Cutting too low leaves wet drywall behind, and cutting too high removes sound board for no reason.

Drying goals for wet walls

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard frames drying around goals: bring the wall cavity back to a dry reference, then confirm it with meters. For drywall, that usually means pulling the wet lower section, flooding the cavity with air movement and re-reading before the wall is closed up. Closing a wall that still reads wet is the shortcut that turns a leak into a mold call.
