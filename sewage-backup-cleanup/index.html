Emergency sewage response

Sewage backup: what to do first

Emergency sewage backup cleanup in Piscataway begins with keeping people away. Raw sewage carries bacteria and pathogens, so keep children, pets and anyone with health concerns out of the affected area. Our line is open 24/7, and we dispatch the same day when available, including nights and weekends.

Do not use sinks, tubs or toilets, because water may be flowing back from the same line. Do not wade through it, and do not run a household vacuum on it. Call the municipal or utility contact if the line in the street is suspected, then call us.

Symptom check

Black water, gray water and what it means

If water is coming up through a floor drain, a toilet or a basement sink and it smells foul, it may be sewage. Restoration professionals call this Category 3 water under ANSI/IICRC S500, sometimes known as black water. It carries pathogens, so the response is different from a clean leak.

A backup can start in your own lateral or in the larger system. Sewer service in Piscataway goes to the Middlesex County Utilities Authority, so a main-line problem may be an authority matter. If the clog is in your own line, a plumber clears it.

Water rising in floor drains

Backflow from the line is reaching the lowest opening.

Gurgling toilets and slow drains

Air and water are being forced back through traps.

Foul odor in the basement

Sewer gas and waste smell are a clear warning.

Several fixtures backing up together

Multiple drains failing points to the main lateral.

The sanitizing process

1

Contain

We isolate the area and wear protective equipment.

2

Remove

Contaminated water is extracted and porous materials such as carpet and padding are removed.

3

Clean and disinfect

Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and disinfected.

4

Dry and verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the space, with readings logged.

Porous materials that touched sewage are generally discarded, not cleaned. That includes carpet, padding, drywall near the waterline and stored cardboard.

Protecting your household until cleanup

The hours between the backup and the cleanup are when people get exposed. Close doors to the affected room, turn off the HVAC fan if ducts run through the area, and keep shoes that touched the water outside. Anyone who touched it should wash with soap and warm water.

Keep people and pets out

Children and animals should stay away until the area is cleaned and verified.

Stop using the plumbing

Flushing and running water feeds the backup, so hold off on fixtures.

Cover or close the area

Close doors and block air movement to limit spread.

Call the right parties

Contact a plumber or the sewer authority for the line and us for cleanup.

Sewage rarely stays in one room. Water that reaches a finished basement can wick into wall bases, stair stringers and stored boxes. We check the full footprint with moisture meters, and we discard porous contents that cannot be cleaned.

Why disinfecting is not enough

Disinfectants work on clean, hard surfaces. They cannot reach through carpet padding or wet drywall, so those materials come out first, then the remaining surfaces are cleaned and treated.
