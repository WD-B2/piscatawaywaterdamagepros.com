Water damage restoration in Highland Park, NJ, starts with the age of the house

Highland Park is a borough of about 14,959 people on the north bank of the Raritan River, roughly a mile from our base on Centennial Avenue in Piscataway. About 80 percent of its homes were built before 1979. That single fact shapes most water damage calls here, because houses of that age often carry original supply piping, older cast-iron drains and plaster or early drywall.

The practical meaning is simple. Pinhole leaks, drain line failures and wall cavities that stay wet unseen are more likely in an older house, so we look past the visible stain with moisture meters and thermal imaging before deciding how much to open.

Mill Brook, the river and heavy rain

Mill Brook runs through the borough, and the Raritan River forms its southern edge. Heavy rain raises both, and low yards and basements take on water. Ida on September 1 and 2, 2021 showed how fast the corridor can flood, and the Raritan crested again in January 2024. Checking an older wall before you cut it

A house built before 1979 may have plaster over wood lath, early gypsum board or paneling over furring strips. Each holds moisture differently, and each reacts differently to drying. Plaster can feel solid while the lath behind it is soaked, and wood lath that stays wet for days can start to soften.

So we map first and cut second. A moisture meter and thermal camera show the wet footprint, and a small inspection opening confirms what the wall is made of. Often the plan is to dry in place, which saves the plaster. When that fails, we cut cleanly, dry the cavity and tell you what to expect for the rebuild.
