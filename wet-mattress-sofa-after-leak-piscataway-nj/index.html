Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak: What to Do in Piscataway

Whether a soaked mattress or sofa can be saved depends on how long it was wet and what kind of water it was.

Homeowner guidePiscataway, NJ

By the Piscataway Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 13, 2026.

The two questions that decide everything

Before you decide to save or toss a wet mattress or sofa, ask two questions. What kind of water was it, and how long has it been wet? Clean supply water, caught within a day, often lets you dry a sofa or a mattress topper. Contaminated water, or anything that has been wet for several days, usually means the item should go.

Those two questions are the same ones restoration crews ask. The standard they follow, ANSI/IICRC S500, sorts water into three categories, and contents handling depends on category.

Save or toss: a quick guide

ItemClean water, caught fastContaminated or old water
Innerspring or foam mattressSometimes dried, but foam holds waterDiscard
Upholstered sofaOften dried and cleanedDiscard

Foam and bedding hold water deep inside, where air reaches slowly. A mattress that looks dry on the surface can stay damp in the core for days. Mattresses also come with warranties that may not cover water damage, so photograph the item and the tag before deciding.

What to do in the first hour

Move it out of the water

Lift it onto blocks or a dry floor, and keep it off wet carpet.

Blot and press

Use towels to draw out water, working from the edges.

Set up airflow

Run a fan and a dehumidifier, keeping the room closed.

Photograph and list

Capture the item, the tag and the damage for any claim.

Do not use a heater or a hair dryer on foam. Heat can damage it, and it dries only the surface. Sunlight can help with a cushion, but only if it is not raining and the item can be moved safely. If you suspect the water was dirty, skip the cleaning and plan to discard the item.

When the water was not clean

Water from a sewer backup, a toilet overflow or an outdoor flood is contaminated, and anything porous that touched it should not be kept. That includes mattresses, upholstery and pillows. Wash non-porous items with detergent and disinfectant, and discard the rest. Sewage backup cleanup explains what we remove and why.

If the item came from a flooded basement after a storm, the same rule applies, and a sofa that sat in outside water for a day is rarely worth saving. For more on how floodwater is treated, see flood damage cleanup.

Insurance and contents

Contents claims depend on the cause of the water, the policy limit and whether the policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, or at least take clear photos with the date. List the age and value. Receipts, bank statements or an online order history help prove the purchase.

If a mattress or sofa was damaged by a covered event, the claim may pay to replace it. If it was damaged by flood, it may not. Check the policy, and ask your agent early.

Basement furniture after heavy rain

Edison recorded at least 12 inches of rain during Ida on September 1 and 2, 2021. Homes that took on water that day lost a lot of basement furniture, sofas and guest-room mattresses. The lesson is to keep upholstered furniture off basement floors in rainy seasons, and to put plastic feet or blocks under anything you cannot move.

Cleaning what you decide to keep

For items that pass the save test, cleaning matters as much as drying. Upholstery cleaners use extraction to pull dirt and moisture out of fabric, and they can remove stains that dry water leaves behind. Wood furniture may need to be wiped, dried slowly and checked for warping or loosened joints.

Dry a cushion on its edge so air reaches both faces, and turn it every few hours. Keep it away from direct heat. Once it feels dry, give it a day more, since cores take longer than the surface.

Smell is the final test. A cushion or a frame that smells sour or musty after drying probably holds moisture or growth inside, and replacing it is better than living with the smell. A restoration crew can handle contents that are bulky or need controlled drying, and can tell you honestly which items are worth the effort.
