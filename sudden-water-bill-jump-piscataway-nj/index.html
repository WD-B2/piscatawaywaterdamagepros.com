What a Sudden Water Bill Jump Means in Piscataway

A bill that doubles with no change in habits usually means a leak. Here is how to find out where, and whose problem it is.

Homeowner guidePiscataway, NJ

By the Piscataway Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 13, 2026.

Start with a ten-minute meter test

A sudden jump in your water bill most often means water is going somewhere you cannot see. The fastest check is the meter. Turn off every fixture and appliance, then read the meter and look at the small leak indicator, which spins when any water flows. If it moves with everything off, you have a leak, either in your plumbing or on the line to it.

A meter that sits still means the water went somewhere in the past, such as a leaking toilet that has stopped or a one-time event like filling a pool.

Toilets cause more bill spikes than any other fixture, because a flapper can leak without a sound. After toilets, supply lines to appliances and the water heater are common sources. A pipe leak inside a wall or under a floor can run for days. If you find water, shut the main and read burst pipe water damage for the next steps.

Whose side is the leak on?

Piscataway's water comes mainly from New Jersey American Water, and the line from the street to your meter and the line from the meter to your house have different owners. In general, the utility is responsible for its main and its side of the meter, and you are responsible for plumbing on your side. A leak on your side can still cause a large bill, so check yours first.

Utility work can also change what you see. A 48-inch main on Possumtown Road leaked on August 2, 2021 and caused a boil-water advisory, and New Jersey American Water began a $2 million upgrade on March 26, 2026. Flushing, shutoffs and pressure changes can shake loose old fittings. If a leak appears right after work in your street, mention it when you call the utility.

If the leak has already caused damage

A hidden leak that ran for weeks can leave wet drywall, swollen trim and a musty smell. Insurance often treats long-term seepage differently from a sudden burst, so photograph what you see before cleanup. The toilet dye test and the meter read, step by step

Two simple tests solve most bill mysteries. For the toilet, lift the tank lid, add a few drops of food coloring to the tank water and wait about fifteen minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper is leaking and the fix is usually a cheap part.

For the meter, find it in the basement, a utility room or a pit near the curb. Lift the lid, wipe the glass and note the dial numbers. Many meters have a small triangle or wheel that spins when water flows. With every fixture off, a moving wheel means a leak. Take a photo, wait an hour and check again.

Write down the numbers, the time and what you tested. A plumber or the utility will want that record, and so will an insurer if the leak has damaged anything.

What to do if you suspect a leak right now

Close the main and check the meter

If the meter keeps moving with every fixture off, the leak is in the line.

Do not dig or cut into walls

Opening a wall without knowing the source can create more damage and a bigger claim.

Look for wet floors and soft spots

A damp patch in a lawn or a warm spot on a floor can point to a hidden line.

Call a plumber and a restoration crew

The plumber fixes the pipe and the crew dries what got wet.
