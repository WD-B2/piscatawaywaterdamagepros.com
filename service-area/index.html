Where we respond

Our base is on Centennial Avenue in Piscataway, and crews dispatch across Middlesex County. Three nearby towns have their own pages with local details, and five Piscataway neighborhoods have theirs: Highland Park about a mile away, Milltown about three miles south and Edison about three miles northeast.

Each page explains local causes, utilities and ZIP codes, so you can see what we know about your town.

Other towns we reach

We also answer calls in Metuchen, Franklin Park, South Bound Brook, South Plainfield, Dunellen and Middlesex, and in New Brunswick, Somerset, North Brunswick, Sayreville, South River, East Brunswick and Perth Amboy. These towns do not have their own pages, but the same 24/7 line and the same process apply.

ZIP codes we serve include 08854, 08904, 08850, 08817, 08820, 08837, 08899, 08840, 08823, 08880 and 07080. Street-delivery addresses in Piscataway use 08854, since 08855 is a PO box ZIP.

How a call from your town is handled

One line serves every town. The dispatcher asks for the street address, so the crew knows which municipality, which water utility and which permit office apply. Crews leave from the Centennial Avenue base in Piscataway, and drive time depends on traffic on I-287, Route 18 and local roads at that hour. We do not quote an arrival time in advance, but the dispatcher will give a realistic window once the address is known.

Highland Park: older homes next to the river

Highland Park sits across the Raritan River and is built mostly from older housing, with roughly 80 percent of homes dating from before 1979. Older supply lines and cast iron drains are common there, so slow leaks inside walls are a frequent cause. The borough is served by Middlesex Water, and borough hall is at 221 S Fifth Ave if a permit question comes up. Call (732) 352-9660 and give the street, and the dispatcher will confirm coverage.

Milltown: runoff and Lawrence Brook

Milltown is small and densely built, with more than 60 percent of its land covered by hard surface, so rain runs off quickly toward Lawrence Brook and the low streets. During Ida, Washington Avenue closed because of flooding. If a basement floods in a heavy storm, the first step is to keep clear of the panel and call, since a sump pump may be the only thing between a damp floor and standing water.

Edison: a large township with four ZIP codes

Edison is the largest town we cover, with 08817, 08820, 08837 and 08899 among its ZIP codes, and water comes from Middlesex Water or New Jersey American Water depending on the street. The township municipal complex is at 100 Municipal Blvd. Ida brought at least 12 inches of rain there, a reminder that storm water can reach homes far from any river.

Nearby towns without their own page

Calls from Metuchen, South Plainfield, Dunellen, Middlesex and New Brunswick are handled the same way as the towns above. The dispatcher confirms the street, the crew brings the same equipment, and the scope depends on the water, not the town line. If your property is rented or part of a building with shared walls, mention that on the call, since a neighboring unit may be involved.

Piscataway neighborhoods with their own pages

Inside the township, five neighborhoods have pages that explain what is specific to them: Lake Nelson, Possumtown, North Stelton, New Market and Raritan Landing. Each one sticks to what we could verify, and says so where we could not.

What changes from town to town

Utilities and permit offices differ. Piscataway's water comes mainly from New Jersey American Water, and sewer service goes through the Middlesex County Utilities Authority. Highland Park is served by the Middlesex Water Company, Milltown runs its own water department and Edison is served by Middlesex Water and New Jersey American Water. We tell you which to call when you describe your address.
