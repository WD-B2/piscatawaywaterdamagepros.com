AC leaks

What AC leak water damage looks like

AC leak water damage in Piscataway comes from condensation, not from the refrigerant. An air conditioner pulls water out of humid air all summer, and that water has to leave through a drain line. When the line clogs, the pan overflows and water soaks whatever is below, often drywall, insulation or a finished closet.

Our line answers 24/7. If the unit is dripping now, switch the system off at the thermostat, so it stops making more water, and place a bucket or towels below. Then call us so the wet materials can be dried before mold sets in.

Where Piscataway AC leaks tend to hide

Summer humidity in central New Jersey keeps condensate flowing for months, and many air handlers sit in attics, closets or basements where a slow drip is easy to miss. The first sign is often a faint stain on a ceiling or a damp spot on a wall near the unit. That small stain can mean the insulation above has been wet for weeks.

Ceiling stain under an attic unit

A brown or yellow patch under an air handler often means the secondary pan overflowed.

Water pooled at the unit

Standing water near an indoor coil or closet unit points to a clogged drain line.

Musty cool air

A dank smell from vents can mean moisture in ducts or around the plenum.

Warped baseboard near the closet

Slow leaks wick into trim and drywall at the base of a wall.

Preventing the next AC leak

Condensate problems are predictable. Drain lines clog with algae and dust, and the clog usually forms months before the overflow. A technician can flush the line, install a float switch that shuts the unit down when the pan fills, and check the pan itself for cracks.

Schedule a seasonal tune-up

A technician can flush the line before the cooling season.

Install a float switch

It turns the unit off if the pan fills.

Look at the pan

A rusted or cracked pan can leak long before it overflows.

Check the attic after heat waves

Long hot spells produce the most condensate.

Attic units are the hardest to watch, so a quick look after a heat wave pays off. A damp stain on the ceiling below is often the first sign, and we see it on spring walk-throughs as often as in summer.

How the standard treats condensate

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard sorts water by contamination level. Fresh supply water is the cleanest category. Condensate that has sat in a drain pan or soaked insulation for days is handled with more caution, because slow, warm, damp conditions let microbes build up. In an attic or closet that has been wet for weeks, we check for that before deciding what stays.

What to do before we arrive: AC leak

Turn the system off

Switch the unit off at the thermostat and the breaker so it stops adding water.

Stay clear of wet wiring

If the leak is near a light fixture or an outlet, do not touch it, and shut that circuit off at the panel only if the panel is dry.

Catch the drip

Place a bucket or towels under the leak and move stored items out of the attic or closet.

Photograph the stain

Take pictures of the ceiling or wall before anything is cut open.
