Water damage in Lake Nelson starts with the age of the house

Lake Nelson sits near the center of Piscataway, close to Metlars Lane, and takes its name from the lake and park at its heart. Byrne Park is close by. Listings for the area show homes built between about 1940 and 2008, mostly with two to four bedrooms, so one street can hold a postwar house next to one from the 1990s.

That spread matters when water gets in. Supply lines, drain material and wall finishes change from one decade to the next, so nobody can tell what sits behind a stained wall from the street. We confirm the material and measure the moisture first, then decide how much to open.

A pinhole leak and a failed supply line look much the same from inside the room. Our burst pipe water damage page explains how we find the source, and the basement water damage cleanup page covers lower levels, which are the rooms people check first after heavy rain.

Safety first

What to do while you wait for a crew

Stop the water

Close the main shutoff or the fixture valve. If you are unsure where the main is, find it before an emergency, not during one.

Keep clear of power

If water has reached outlets, the panel or appliances, stay out of the room. Call the utility or 911 for sparks, a burning smell or gas.

Avoid outside water

Water that came in from a yard, street or drain may be contaminated. Keep children and pets away from it.

Photograph first

Take wide and close photos with the time before you move things. They help any insurance claim.

Drying, mold and insurance in plain terms

The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. The industry reference for this work is the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. That is why we remove standing water first with water extraction and then measure moisture in the walls, floors and ceilings instead of judging by touch. Structural drying and dehumidification shows how the readings decide when equipment leaves.

On insurance, many policies cover a sudden event such as a burst pipe and exclude flood or long-term seepage. Your policy and your insurer decide, so call them early and keep the photos and the moisture log. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified.
