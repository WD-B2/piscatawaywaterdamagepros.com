Documenting storm damage for a claim

Storm claims move faster when the evidence is clear. Take wide photos of each wet room, then close-ups of stains, drips and wet materials, and write down the time rain started and when you first noticed water. If you must remove items, keep a list of what was discarded.

Photograph the entry point

Capture the window, ceiling or wall where water entered, before any cleanup.

Record the weather

Note the date and time of the storm, which links the loss to a covered event.

Keep damaged items

Store a sample of discarded materials until the adjuster has seen the claim.

Save every receipt

Emergency tarps, repairs and equipment are often reimbursable.

In New Jersey, insurers have deadlines to acknowledge and investigate claims. The state's rules at N.J.A.C. 11:2-17 call for acknowledgment within 10 working days and investigation within 10 working days, with a decision due within 30 calendar days of proof of loss. A clear photo record keeps that clock simple.

Wind-driven rain versus flood

Rain that enters through a roof or window is usually treated as storm damage under a homeowners policy. Water that rises from the ground or from a river is flood, which standard policies exclude.

Getting ready before the next storm

A few steps before storm season limit surprises. Clear gutters and downspouts, check that grading directs water away from the foundation, and test your sump pump. Take dated photos of your home's condition now, since before-and-after images support a claim.

What to do before a crew reaches the storm damage

Stay away from downed lines

If a power line is down or the service drop is damaged, call the utility and keep clear.

Treat storm water as contaminated

Rain that came through a roof is cleaner than runoff from the yard, but neither is safe to wade in near electricity.

Tarp what you can

A temporary tarp on a roof opening limits more rain. Do not climb a wet roof to do it.

Photograph the intrusion points

Windows, flashing and walls tell the insurer where water got in.
