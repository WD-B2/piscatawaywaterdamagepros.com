Kitchen leak damage

Water under the sink and behind the cabinets

Kitchen water damage in Piscataway usually starts with a slow leak at a sink supply, a dishwasher line or a refrigerator ice maker. The leak sits under cabinets and behind toe-kicks, so the damage can be well advanced before you see a stain.

Our line answers 24/7, and we dispatch the same day when available. Shut off the water to the fixture, empty the cabinet and photograph the damage.

What water does in a kitchen

Kitchens combine several water-sensitive materials: particleboard cabinets, laminate or wood flooring, drywall and often a subfloor with a tile layer. Particleboard swells permanently when it absorbs water, while solid wood and plywood dry better.

Swollen cabinet base

Particleboard bulges and crumbles at the bottom.

Cupped flooring near the sink

Wood and laminate lift near the source.

Musty cabinet odor

Dampness behind the toe-kick keeps the smell alive.

Loose tile or soft spots

A wet subfloor weakens the tile bed.

The hidden path of kitchen water

Water in a kitchen rarely stays where it starts. A dishwasher line that leaks at the back can run under the cabinet run, soak the subfloor and appear as a stain on the ceiling of the room below. In a two-story townhouse or condo the path is even longer, so we check the room underneath.

Sink cabinets hide most slow leaks. If the shelf at the bottom feels soft or the cabinet back has a tide mark, the leak has been there a while. Piscataway kitchens in Rutgers-area rentals and older ranch homes often have particleboard cabinet bases, which makes early detection valuable.

Empty the cabinet

Remove everything so air can move and you can see the shelf and the back panel.

Dry the surface

Blot standing water and leave the doors open, but skip the hair dryer on swollen board.

Note the age of the fixture

Supply hoses and shutoff valves fail with age, and the date helps in a claim.

Photograph the base

Take photos of the toe-kick, the flooring edge and the wall behind the appliance.

When a countertop has to come off

Stone and laminate tops are usually left in place. If water has reached the substrate below the sink cutout or a dishwasher bracket, the top may be detached to dry the cabinet underneath and then reset.

What the S500 standard means for cabinets and floors

Kitchen losses often start as clean-water leaks from a dishwasher line or sink supply, which ANSI/IICRC S500 treats as the cleanest category. That is good news for the cabinets, because clean-water cases are the ones most likely to dry in place. A toe-kick that is opened early dries faster than a sealed one, and the moisture readings tell us when the cavity behind it is done.
