Raritan Landing was built on a river, then moved uphill after floods

Raritan Landing was a colonial port community on the Raritan River that thrived from the 1720s. Floods pushed some merchants to build on higher ground. Peter Bodine's house of 1728 on River Road near Metlars Lane and Cornelius Low's house of 1741 on the bluff at Landing Lane both survive as examples. Today the old landing lies along River Road beside Johnson Park, and East Jersey Old Town Village at 1050 River Road tells the story.

That history shows this stretch of the river has flooded before. It is not proof about any house today, and we do not claim it is.

River Road and big storms

Ida arrived on September 1, 2021, and a Piscataway resident died on River Road that day, according to a 2026 stormwater presentation. The safety rule follows plainly: never walk or drive through water on River Road or any low road. For property, a New Jersey Department of Community Affairs notice places one River Road address within the 100-year floodplain. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check your own address on FEMA's flood map service before you decide about flood cover.

Outside water means contaminated water

Under ANSI/IICRC S500, river and storm water are treated as contaminated. That decides the work. Wet carpet, padding, insulation and the base of drywall usually come out, and the structure is cleaned and dried. The steps are on our flood damage cleanup page, drain-backed water is covered under sewage backup cleanup, and water extraction begins the work.

Stay out of moving water

Do not walk or drive through water on River Road or any low road. It can hide a washed-out surface.

Leave the basement if water is rising

A wet panel or furnace can electrify the water. Wait for the utility.

Do not touch wet electronics

Insurance after river or storm water

Standard homeowners policies commonly exclude flood, while a sudden burst pipe is often covered. Flood cover comes from the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood policy, and Piscataway has been in the NFIP since January 18, 1984. Your insurer decides coverage, so call early, keep photographs and keep the moisture log. Receipts for emergency work, the list of damaged contents and a dated record of each call to your insurer all belong in the claim file.

Drying and mold

The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, because mold can grow on damp ones. We confirm dryness with readings in the wall and floor, as described under structural drying and dehumidification. We make no health claims about mold.

After river water we also check what it touched: insulation, the bottom of wall cavities and flooring layers that trap moisture. Cabinets and trim can often be saved when the water was shallow and left quickly. Contents that cannot be cleaned are listed one by one, so you can include them in an insurance claim.
