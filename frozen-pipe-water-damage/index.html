Emergency frozen pipe response

When frozen pipes thaw and flood

Emergency frozen pipe water damage in Piscataway often arrives when the weather warms. A pipe that froze and split stays plugged with ice until it thaws, then water pours from the crack. Our line is open 24/7, and crews dispatch the same day when available, including nights and weekends during cold snaps.

If you find a frozen pipe, close the main valve before it thaws, open the faucet and leave the area dry. Do not use an open flame to thaw it. If a pipe has burst, shut off the main, shut off power only if safe and call us.

How freezing affects Piscataway pipes

NOAA data puts New Jersey at about 179 freeze-free days a year, which leaves a long stretch of freezing nights from late fall into spring. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated garages, attics and crawl spaces are the most exposed. Piscataway homes built before modern insulation standards often have supply lines in these areas.

Pipes usually split where ice has built pressure between a blockage and a closed tap. The split can be a small slit, so water may not show until the thaw.

No water or a trickle from a tap

Ice blocks the line, which is a warning before the split shows.

Frost on a visible pipe

Frost means the pipe surface is below freezing.

Bulging or cracked pipe

A bulge shows where ice has expanded the pipe wall.

Wet spot after warming

A stain on a ceiling or wall when it warms can show a thawed split.

What the cleanup involves

1

Shut and extract

The main is off, and standing water is removed.

Meters and thermal imaging trace water through walls and ceilings, then we open them.

3

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the cavity, insulation is replaced as needed.

A plumber repairs the pipe and we rebuild the finish.

Where frozen pipes hide in Piscataway homes

The riskiest pipes are the ones nobody looks at. Lines in exterior walls, over unheated garages, in attic knee walls and in crawl spaces see the lowest temperatures. A kitchen sink on an outside wall is a common culprit, because cabinet doors keep warm air away from the pipes.

Open cabinet doors

Let heated room air reach sink lines on exterior walls during a freeze.

Let a tap drip

A slow drip relieves pressure between an ice plug and the tap.

Seal drafts

Close gaps around pipes where they pass through walls and rims.

Know your main valve

Everyone in the home should know where it is and how to close it.

After the thaw

Check ceilings and wall bases when the temperature rises. A pipe that cracked during the freeze may show no water until the ice melts, and the first sign can be a stain several hours later.
