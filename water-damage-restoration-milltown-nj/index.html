Flooding is the first water problem in Milltown, NJ

Milltown is a small borough of about 6,968 people, roughly three miles south of our Piscataway base. Water damage restoration here begins with the ground, because Lawrence Brook runs through the borough and more than 60 percent of the land is covered by hard surfaces. Rain that cannot soak in runs into streets, yards and basements.

That mix explains why flooding and basement dry-out lead our Milltown calls. We pump, remove wet materials, dry the structure and treat for mold risk, and we start by asking where the water came from.

What Ida taught Milltown

During Ida on September 1 and 2, 2021, Washington Avenue was closed by flooding, and the storm brought water to many Milltown properties. The takeaway is access. When a main street closes, crews and equipment take longer, so early calls matter. Call when the rain starts, not when the basement is full.

Lawrence Brook is the reason many residents carry flood questions into insurance conversations. After the water goes down

Once pumps shut off, the real work begins. A basement that looks dry can still hold water in block walls, under carpet and in the lower few inches of drywall. Mold can begin within a day or two in humid conditions, which is why we keep drying equipment running until readings match a dry reference.

Brook flooding also leaves silt. A thin gray film on a floor is sediment from the brook, and it carries bacteria. We wash and treat hard surfaces and discard porous materials that cannot be disinfected. For contaminated carpet, padding is always removed.
