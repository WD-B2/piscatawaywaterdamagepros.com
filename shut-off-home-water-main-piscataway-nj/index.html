How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Piscataway

Knowing where your main valve is takes five minutes and can save a flooded floor. Here is how to find it and use it.

Homeowner guidePiscataway, NJ
24/7 emergency line(732) 352-9660 Call now

By the Piscataway Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 13, 2026.

Find it before you need it

The home water main shutoff stops water to the entire house, and it is the first step in almost every pipe emergency. Most homes have it near where the water line enters the building, often in a basement, a utility room or a crawl space, near the water meter. Find yours today, make sure it turns and label it with tape.

If you have never touched it, test it gently. Old valves can stick or leak when moved, and it is better to learn that on a calm afternoon than in the middle of a flood.

Two kinds of valves

Valve typeHow it worksTip
Gate valve (round handle)Turn clockwise several times to closeDo not force it, and exercise it yearly
Ball valve (lever handle)A quarter turn closes it, with the lever across the pipeEasier to use in an emergency
Curb stop at the streetOwned by the utilityDo not operate it yourself

Gate valves are common in older homes and tend to seize, while ball valves are common in newer installations and in replacements. If your gate valve does not fully close, ask a plumber to replace it with a ball valve. A valve that does not stop the water is a risk.

What to do when a pipe lets go

1

Close the main

Turn the handle clockwise or give the lever a quarter turn.

2

Open a low faucet

Drain what remains in the lines to reduce leakage.

3

Make it safe

Keep clear of wet outlets, and switch off power only if the panel is dry and reachable.

4

Call for help

Call a plumber for the pipe and a restoration crew for the water.

If you cannot find or close the valve, call the utility. In Piscataway, most homes are supplied by New Jersey American Water, which can advise on the street-side shutoff. The curb stop belongs to the utility, so let them operate it.

What not to do

Do not force a stuck valve

Breaking it can make the flood worse.

Do not touch the utility's meter shutoff

That valve belongs to the water company.

Do not stand in water near electrical panels

Wait for guidance or call the electric utility.

Do not turn the water back on until a plumber says so

A repaired pipe needs a pressure check.

Appliances have their own shutoffs under sinks and behind washers. These stops are useful when only one fixture is leaking. Test them each year, and replace rubber hoses with braided steel ones. If a fixture leak has already wetted the floor, appliance leak water damage explains how we dry it.

What happens after the water stops

Once the main is closed, the damage is limited but not over. Water that has already escaped keeps wicking into drywall and wood. Call for extraction and drying as soon as the source is controlled. Our burst pipe water damage page explains what the cleanup involves and what a plumber will do.

Street-side problems belong to the utility

A 48-inch main on Possumtown Road leaked on August 2, 2021 and led to a boil-water advisory. That was the utility's pipe, and the utility's repair. Sewer service goes through the Middlesex County Utilities Authority. If the problem is in the street, in the meter pit or on the utility's side of the meter, call the water company. If it is inside your house, close your main.

If you are near Edison or Highland Park, the utility may differ, since Middlesex Water serves parts of both. Our line is at (732) 352-9660 for any help with the water itself.

Make shutoff a household habit

Everyone old enough to help should know where the main is and how it works. Walk each family member to the valve once a year, have them turn it and open a tap to see the water stop. Put a tag on the valve that says which way closes it, and put a flashlight nearby, because basements are dark when the power goes out.

If you are leaving for more than a few days, close the main and drain a low faucet. In winter, also leave the heat on at a safe setting and ask someone to check the house. A burst pipe in an empty house can run for days before anyone notices.

Consider a smart shutoff valve or leak sensors if you travel often or have a finished basement. These devices detect water and can close the main automatically, and they are affordable compared with a flooded floor. A plumber can install one, and your insurer may offer a discount for it.

Finally, keep a plumber's number and ours in your phone. In an emergency, you want the next call to take ten seconds.
