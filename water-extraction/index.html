Emergency water removal

When standing water is an emergency

Emergency water extraction in Piscataway means getting standing water off floors and out of wall cavities before it soaks into the structure. Water extraction is the first step of restoration, and the sooner it happens the less drying and rebuilding follows. Our line answers 24/7, and crews dispatch the same day when available, including nights and weekends.

Before we arrive, stop the source if you can. Close the main shutoff, lift furniture legs onto foil or blocks, and move rugs and electronics out of the wet area. Stay out of the room if water has reached outlets or the panel.

How water extraction actually works

Extraction is mechanical, not magic. Submersible pumps clear deep water, then truck-mounted and portable wand extractors pull water out of carpet, padding and the top layer of floors. The goal is to remove as much liquid as possible before anything is evaporated, because liquid water is far cheaper to extract than to dry.

1

Find the limits

Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where water traveled beyond the visible puddle.

2

Pump and extract

Pumps clear depth, then wand units work the carpet and floors in overlapping passes.

3

Lift or cut what is needed

Padding is usually removed. Carpet may be lifted, dried and reinstalled depending on the water category.

4

Hand off to drying

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers take over, with readings logged until the structure is dry.

Piscataway water sources we see most

Most homes here are supplied by New Jersey American Water, and a supply line failure can put gallons per minute on a floor. A 48-inch main on Possumtown Road leaked on August 2, 2021 and led to a boil-water advisory. A burst water main in the street is a utility job, but water that enters a house from a failed fitting inside is ours to extract.

Sewer service goes through the Middlesex County Utilities Authority, so a backup into a basement floor drain is handled differently, as sewage. See sewage backup cleanup if the water smells or looks dark, because that changes the safety plan.
