Slab leak symptoms

Warm spots on the floor or a high water bill?

If a floor feels warm, the water meter spins with every tap off or you hear running water under the house, you may have water leaking from a line under or inside a concrete slab. Slab leak water damage in Piscataway is the moisture that line releases into flooring, walls and the soil around the foundation.

The plumbing trade calls it a slab leak. Our line answers 24/7, and we dispatch the same day when available. If you suspect one, shut off the main water valve and call a plumber for detection.

Emergency

Emergency slab leak response

A slab leak becomes an emergency when water reaches the surface, pools in a room or damages flooring. Turn off the main, move rugs and furniture, and call us. Do not walk through water near outlets. After hours and on weekends, our crews dispatch when available, so call even at night.

Slab construction in Piscataway

Many Piscataway homes have basements, but slab-on-grade is common in garden apartments, additions and some townhouses. Where slab construction is used, pipes may run in or beneath the concrete, and a leak there can go unnoticed for weeks. We do not assume a slab. If your home has a basement or crawl space, the water more likely came from another source.

What we do and what a plumber does

Leak detection and repair belong to a licensed plumber, who uses acoustic and thermal tools to find the line. We handle the water damage: extraction, drying, flooring removal where needed and repair after the line is fixed.

Checking whether your water use looks normal

A simple meter test helps before you call anyone. Turn off every fixture and appliance, then look at the meter's small leak indicator. If it keeps moving, water is going somewhere. Take a photo of the reading, wait an hour, and photograph it again.

Turn everything off

Shut off taps, ice makers and irrigation so only a leak can move the meter.

Read the meter twice

Take photos an hour apart and compare the numbers.

Listen at floor level

Put an ear near the floor and listen for a hiss near the water heater and bathrooms.

Why we do not guess at the source

Finding a slab leak depends on acoustic and thermal equipment, and guessing can lead to opening floors in the wrong place. We follow the plumber's findings and dry the damage once the line is isolated.

Where S500 stops and the plumber starts

ANSI/IICRC S500 governs how water is categorized and how drying is verified. It does not repair the pipe. A slab leak needs a plumber to find and fix the line, and we dry the floors, walls and cabinets around it to the standard's dry reference. Doing the two jobs in the right order keeps the structure from being dried twice.
