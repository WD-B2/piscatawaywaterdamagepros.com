Why water heaters fail and where the water goes

Tank water heaters corrode from the inside and eventually leak at the bottom or at fittings. A tank that holds 40 to 80 gallons can empty onto a basement floor in minutes if the seam fails. Water heaters often sit in basements, utility closets and garages, where the water spreads under walls and into finished spaces.

Moisture or rust at the tank base

A rusty stain on the floor can mean a slow leak.

Dripping relief valve

Rumbling or popping sounds

Sediment buildup can overheat the tank bottom.

Warped flooring near the unit

Slow leaks quietly swell floor materials.

Safety steps for a leaking tank

A leaking tank mixes water with gas and electricity, so the first steps are about safety before cleanup. Shutting the cold-water supply stops the refill, and turning off the fuel or power stops the heating element or burner from running in a half-empty tank. A tank full of hot water can also scald, so keep children and pets back.

Shut off the cold supply

The valve sits on the pipe feeding the top of the tank. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Cut power or gas

Switch off the breaker for an electric unit or set a gas unit's control to the off or pilot position.

Do not drain it yet

Wait for a plumber unless the tank is already empty, because draining hot water is a scald risk.

Photograph the data label

The label lists the model and age, which an insurer or plumber will ask for.

Utility closets inside finished homes deserve extra care. Drywall and carpet next to the closet wick water outward, and the stain may show up two rooms away. We check walls on every side of the unit, and the floor of any room below it.

Why a small drip matters

A slow drip from a relief valve or a fitting can wet insulation and subfloor for weeks. It often shows first as rust at the base of the tank, which is a sign to call a plumber before the next failure.

Why heater water is usually the easy category

A leaking tank usually releases clean supply water, which ANSI/IICRC S500 places in its least contaminated category. That is why many water heater floods are saved with extraction and drying alone. The category changes if the water sat for days or crossed a dirty area, so the first photos and the time since the leak started both matter.
